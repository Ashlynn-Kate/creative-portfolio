# Conceptual portrait category and curated role tags

## Context

ADR-0047 clarified Ashlynn's roles and project contributions but intentionally
left the Photography browse tag in place for a later category-language review.
That review established that the portraits begin with a concept rather than a
photography service. Ashlynn photographs and edits her own portraits, but does
not currently seek commissions to photograph other people. She does welcome
styling work, even though it is not a primary focus. This decision resolves
the deferred tag question and supersedes ADR-0047 while preserving its other
role and credit decisions.

## Decision

Use “Conceptual portraits” for the area of work, Object Translations type, and
the About introduction. “Editorial” may describe the visual sensibility, but
is not needed as the category or repeated across project copy. Keep Film and
Live performance as the other areas of work.

Use browse tags for meaningful contributions that Ashlynn would welcome
visitors exploring as potential work, rather than every task performed. Keep
Creative Direction and Styling on Object Translations, remove Photography from
its browse tags, and explicitly retain her planning, direction, photography,
editing, styling, and modeling credits in the project narrative. Frame
photography as part of realizing her own concepts, not as a separate service
offering.

Continue the role language established in ADR-0047: the site-wide introduction
names creative and artistic direction, choreography, and performance; the About
biography describes performance in evergreen terms; and case studies distinguish
Ashlynn's sole work from co-direction and work developed with collaborators.
Object Translations remains credited as her own series. Mindspace credits her
original concept and lead performance, while direction and choreography are
shared with Breana. The Prince of Egypt credits her story, casting, music
selection and edits, costumes, and choreography, while describing set and
lighting work as collaboration with their teams.

## Options considered

- **Editorial portraits:** evokes the intended feel, but can suggest a
  photography or publication category rather than concept development.
- **Photography browse tag:** accurately records a task, but could imply an
  opportunity she does not want to advertise.
- **Conceptual portraits with curated role tags (chosen):** names the work by
  its driving idea while project copy gives full, accurate credits.

## Trade-offs

- The browse tags are not an exhaustive credits list; visitors must read the
  project narrative for every contribution.
- “Conceptual portraits” still describes the resulting images, so the case
  narrative must show how each concept is developed.

## Revisit if

- Ashlynn begins offering photography commissions or changes the emphasis of
  the portrait series.
- New work calls for additional areas of work or browse roles.
