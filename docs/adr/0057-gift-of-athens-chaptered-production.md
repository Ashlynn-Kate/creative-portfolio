# Gift of Athens chaptered production page

## Context
The Gift of Athens is a substantial full-length ballet with historical context, scene narratives, production-process details, video excerpts, and community impact. Hiding most of that material in disclosures would obscure Ashlynn's work, while presenting it as an uninterrupted wall of prose would make the long story difficult to navigate. The owner chose the chaptered Option B prototype as the preferred structure.

## Decision
- Present the production photo beside its title, summary, credits, and disciplines at the top of the standalone case study.
- Follow the introduction with a visual index linking to the visible Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle chapters.
- Keep the creation process and rehearsal footage together before the story chapters.
- Keep scene videos with their relevant chapter narratives and use Google Drive thumbnail links, consistent with ADR-0008.
- Close with the community-impact story and the memorial and veterans-group photographs, displayed without cropping their source proportions.
- List The Gift of Athens alongside The Prince of Egypt under Live performance → Full-length productions.

## Options considered
- **One continuous story:** preserves every detail but makes it harder to find a particular philosopher or scene.
- **Expandable chapter disclosures:** shortens the initial page, but hides the context and resources the owner wants visitors to see.
- **Visible chapters with an image-led index (chosen):** keeps the complete story available while making its three-part structure easy to scan and navigate.

## Trade-offs
**Pros**
- The page communicates the scope of the production quickly and provides direct ways into each philosopher's story.
- Process, scene footage, and community impact remain visible rather than hidden behind multiple clicks.
- The chapters can grow as additional production context is added.

**Cons**
- The case study is intentionally long; the index and visual breaks reduce, but do not remove, the need to scroll.
- Each video still opens in Google Drive until the project adopts the approved embedded-video approach.

## Revisit if
- The owner adds substantial new chapters or materials that make the current single-page structure difficult to scan.
- Rehearsal or scene footage moves from Drive to an approved embedded-video platform.
